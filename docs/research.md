# Research: which patients to focus on

Goal: find the largest patient groups where problems after discharge are common and often missed, and where home monitoring is shown to help. The platform stays generic (any rehabilitacija from a template), but the triage rules, demo and pitch are tuned for the main niche.

Figures below come from abstracts and summaries. Open the full paper before quoting a number on stage.

## Summary

| Rank | Group | Why | Home signal | Evidence that monitoring helps |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Heart failure** | Most common reason for readmission; ~half readmitted within a year; 20,000–40,000 patients in Slovenia | **Weight** (objective), swelling, breathlessness | **Strong**: Cochrane review + TIM-HF2 RCT |
| 2 | COPD after an exacerbation | 35% readmitted within 90 days in Europe; elderly | Breathlessness, cough/sputum, (SpO₂ if oximeter) | Moderate: meta-analysis RR 0.74 for exacerbation readmissions |
| 2b | Stroke | 17.4% readmitted within 30 days; infection, heart, new stroke | Blood pressure, new symptoms, pills, falls | **Strong** for care continuing at home (Cochrane) |
| 2c | Cancer on chemotherapy at home | Symptoms between cycles are often missed | Temperature, nausea, diarrhoea, pain | **Strong**: Basch RCT (fewer ER visits, longer survival) |
| 3 | Older adults on many medicines (any diagnosis) | Drug problems are the most common harm after discharge; many preventable | Pills taken / missed, side effects | Indirect: medication reviews and reminders |
| — | Sepsis and pneumonia survivors | High readmission rates, but no clear home signal | Temperature, general state | Weak |
| 1b | After a heart attack or heart surgery | Common, severe; rehab and medicine adherence decide the outcome | Chest pain, breathlessness, wound, pills | **Strong** for (home) cardiac rehab |
| 1c | Hip/knee replacement | Very common; 90-day readmission ~6–10%, mostly infections and medical causes | Wound, temperature, calf swelling, pain | Weak for monitoring, strong case for rehab adherence |

**Decision proposal:** six templates for severe conditions: **heart failure** (main niche, demo story), **after a heart attack or heart surgery**, **hip or knee replacement**, **COPD**, **stroke** and **cancer on chemotherapy at home**. The medication layer (group 3) applies to every rehabilitacija. Each group has at least one systematic review or RCT below that can be shown on a slide.

| Group | Key paper to present |
|---|---|
| Heart failure | Cochrane CD007228 (2015): telemonitoring, deaths RR 0.80, heart-failure admissions RR 0.71 |
| After heart attack / heart surgery | Cochrane CD007130 (2023): home cardiac rehab as effective as centre-based |
| Hip/knee replacement | Readmission and cost studies (section 1c); rehab adherence |
| COPD | Lu et al. 2021 meta-analysis: telemonitoring, exacerbation readmissions RR 0.74 |

## Chosen templates (proposal)

Six severe-condition templates, all built the same way. Each template defines its check-in questions, red-flag rules, medicines and rehab plan:

| Template | Who | Daily check-in | Red flags | Rehab |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Srčno popuščanje** (main) | Heart failure after a hospital stay | Weight, ankle swelling, breathlessness (incl. at night), pills | Weight +2 kg in 3 days or above dry weight; worse breathlessness; missed diuretic | Daily walking, minutes increasing |
| **Po srčnem infarktu ali operaciji srca** | Heart attack, stent, bypass, valve surgery | Chest pain, breathlessness, pills, wound (after surgery), activity | Any chest pain → 112 at once; missed antiplatelet pills; red or oozing wound; fever | Home cardiac rehab: walking plan + light exercises |
| **Zamenjava kolka ali kolena** | Hip or knee replacement | Pain, temperature, wound photo, calf swelling, exercises | Fever >38 °C; red, warm or oozing wound; one calf swollen or painful (possible clot); pain rising over days | Physio exercises with videos |

| **KOPB** (COPD) | After a hospital stay for a COPD flare-up | Breathlessness, cough and sputum colour, inhaler use, fever | Much worse breathlessness; sputum turns green or yellow; fever; can't speak in full sentences → 112 | Breathing exercises + daily walking |
| **Možganska kap** (stroke) | After a stroke or TIA | Blood pressure, new weakness or speech problems, pills, falls, swallowing, mood | Any new face/arm/speech symptom → 112 at once (GROM); high blood pressure; missed blood thinners; a fall | Home rehab exercises, mostly mobility |
| **Rak na sistemskem zdravljenju** (cancer, chemotherapy at home) | Patients sent home between chemotherapy cycles | Temperature, nausea/vomiting, diarrhoea, pain, fatigue, eating, mood | Fever ≥38 °C → call the oncology ward at once (risk of neutropenic infection); can't drink; severe diarrhoea or pain | Light activity; emotional support screen |

**For every template:**
- Pill reminders with "Vzeto". Medicine problems are the most common harm after discharge (section 3).
- **zDoma is not an emergency service.** Chest pain, sudden severe breathlessness or fainting always shows a full-screen "Pokličite 112" first, before anything is sent to staff.
## 1. Heart failure (srčno popuščanje)

**Size and burden**
- Medicare, 11.9 million discharges: 19.6% readmitted within 30 days, 34.0% within 90 days. Heart failure is the most common medical cause of readmission, ahead of pneumonia and COPD. — Jencks, Williams, Coleman. *N Engl J Med* 2009;360:1418–28. [PubMed 19339721](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19339721/)
- After a heart failure admission, 35.2% of readmissions are for heart failure again (vs 10.0% after a heart attack, 22.4% after pneumonia). Patients keep coming back for the same, preventable reason. — [PMC3688083](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3688083)
- Europe: almost half of patients hospitalised for acute heart failure are readmitted within a year. — Karolinska Institutet, 2026. [News release](https://news.ki.se/high-risk-of-readmission-and-death-among-heart-failure-patients)
- 30-day readmission rates in Europe vary with the method: 6.2% in an Italian registry ([PubMed 26018852](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26018852/)), 20.3% in a Swedish cohort ([PMC7326238](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7326238)). The method alone can change the rate more than two-fold. **Use the 1-year figure in the pitch.**
- Slovenia: about 20,000–40,000 patients ([ZD Velenje leaflet](https://www.zd-velenje.si/wp-content/assets/zzdm/02/IV-25.pdf); UKC Ljubljana via [Delo](https://www.delo.si/polet/neozdravljiva-bolezen-srca)). One of the most common causes of cardiovascular hospitalisation ([NIJZ yearbook 2024](https://nijz.si/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/2.4.1_SZB_2024_Z.pdf)). **No national readmission rate found yet.**

**Evidence that home monitoring helps**
- **Cochrane review, 41 studies** (Inglis et al. 2015, CD007228). Non-invasive telemonitoring: all-cause mortality RR 0.80 (95% CI 0.68–0.94), heart-failure hospitalisation RR 0.71 (0.60–0.83). Structured telephone support: mortality RR 0.87, heart-failure hospitalisation RR 0.85. **Neither reduced all-cause hospitalisation.** — [PubMed 26517969](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26517969/), [Cochrane](https://www.cochrane.org/CD007228)
- **TIM-HF2 RCT** (Koehler et al., *Lancet* 2018; 1,571 patients, Germany). Daily transmission of weight, blood pressure and ECG to a telemedicine centre. Days lost to unplanned cardiovascular admissions or death: 4.88% vs 6.64%. All-cause death: 7.86 vs 11.34 per 100 person-years (HR 0.70). — [PubMed 30153985](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30153985/)
- The benefit of TIM-HF2 **disappeared one year after the monitoring stopped**. — [TUM follow-up](https://portal.fis.tum.de/en/publications/mortality-and-morbidity-1-year-after-stopping-a-remote-patient-ma/)

**The warning sign**
- ESC patient guidance: tell your doctor or nurse if your weight goes up by **more than 2 kg in 3 days**. — [heartfailurematters.org (ESC)](https://www.heartfailurematters.org/warning-signs/rapid-weight-gain/)
- Some sources use 2 kg in 2 days. The exact rule should be confirmed in the ESC 2021 guideline and 2023 update, or with a cardiologist.

## 1b. After a heart attack or heart surgery

- **Cochrane review of exercise-based cardiac rehabilitation** (Dibben et al. 2021, CD001800; evidence to Sept 2020): reduces all-cause death (RR 0.87 at 6–12 months), heart attacks and hospital admissions, and improves quality of life. — [PubMed 34741536](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34741536/), [Cochrane](https://www.cochrane.org/CD001800)
- A newer update (107 RCTs, 26,886 people) reports **28% fewer heart attacks and 35% fewer all-cause hospital admissions**. — [University of Glasgow, 2026](https://www.gla.ac.uk/news/headline_1291689_en.html). Quote this one with the newer review as the source, not Dibben 2021.
- **Home-based cardiac rehab works as well as centre-based**: similar deaths, exercise capacity and quality of life. The 2023 version explicitly includes digital and telehealth programmes. Adherence was slightly better at home in earlier versions. — Cochrane CD007130.pub5, 2023. [Cochrane](https://www.cochrane.org/CD007130/VASC_home-based-versus-supervised-centre-based-cardiac-rehabilitation)
- **For zDoma:** this is the strongest support for the rehab feature. A home cardiac rehab plan in an app is the kind of programme the review covers.

## 1c. Hip or knee replacement

- 90-day readmissions after hip replacement: 5.6% in one large institutional study, three-quarters of them for medical rather than orthopaedic reasons. — [PubMed 38797449](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38797449/)
- A meta-analysis found 7.7% after hip and 9.7% after knee replacement at 90 days. Surgical site infection was the main cause after knee replacement (source to confirm before quoting).
- Infections, dislocations and fractures around the implant cause 59% of 90-day readmission costs after hip replacement. — [PubMed 30755375](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30755375/)
- Large volume of patients, but a lower readmission rate than the heart templates. Its value is wound and infection checks plus rehab adherence.

## 2. COPD after an exacerbation

- One of the most common medical causes of readmission (Jencks 2009, above).
- Meta-analysis of 17 RCTs (3,001 patients with an exacerbation in the past year): telemonitoring lowered exacerbation-related readmissions (RR 0.74, 95% CI 0.60–0.92) and emergency visits. Monitoring for 12 months or longer worked better. — Lu et al., *Front Med* 2021. [PMC8460761](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8460761)
- For 30-day readmissions specifically, results are mixed: only 4 of 12 RCTs showed an effect. — Mishra et al., *BMC Digital Health* 2024. [Link](https://link.springer.com/10.1186/s44247-024-00103-x)
- Reviews of heart failure and COPD together find that telemonitoring can reduce the readmission burden. — Stergiopoulos et al., *Front Digit Health* 2024. [PDF](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/digital-health/articles/10.3389/fdgth.2024.1441334/pdf)
- **How often they come back:** the European COPD Audit (>400 hospitals) found **35.1% readmitted within 90 days**. — [PubMed 26493806](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26493806/). A patient-level meta-analysis of ~66,000 patients: 7.1% at 30 days, 12.6% at 90 days, **32.1% within a year**. — [PMC7211949](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7211949)

## 2b. Stroke (možganska kap)

- **30-day readmission 17.4%** (meta-analysis of 10 studies, 253,680 patients). Leading causes: infection, coronary heart disease, recurrent stroke. — Zhong et al., *Neurol Sci* 2016. [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10072-016-2570-5)
- **Early supported discharge** (care continues at home with a coordinated team): death or dependency **OR 0.80** (95% CI 0.67–0.95; 16 trials, 2,359 patients), about 5 fewer bad outcomes per 100 patients, hospital stay about 5.5 days shorter, **no increase in readmissions**. — Langhorne & Baylan, Cochrane CD000443, 2017. [PubMed 28703869](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28703869/)
- **Telerehabilitation** after stroke is not worse than in-person rehab (22 trials, 1,937 patients), and may cost less. Evidence is low to moderate. — Laver et al., Cochrane CD010255, 2020. [Cochrane](https://www.cochrane.org/CD010255)
- **For zDoma:** a home check-in fits the known causes (infection, heart problems, new stroke). New face/arm/speech symptoms must always go to 112 first.

## 2c. Cancer: chemotherapy at home (rak na sistemskem zdravljenju)

- **RCT, 766 patients starting chemotherapy** for metastatic cancer, at Memorial Sloan Kettering. Patients reported symptoms online between visits, and nurses got an email alert when a symptom got worse. Fewer emergency visits and better quality of life (Basch et al., *J Clin Oncol* 2016).
- **Survival 31.2 vs 26.0 months** (P = .03). This was a post hoc analysis from one centre, published as a research letter, so present it as promising, not proven. — Basch et al., *JAMA* 2017;318(2):197–198. [PMC5817466](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5817466/), [MSK press release](https://www.mskcc.org/news-releases/real-time-patient-reported-symptom-monitoring-demonstrates-overall-survival-benefit)
- **For zDoma:** this is the closest published match to the core loop: patient reports symptoms → system flags a worsening one → a nurse acts. It also covers the emotional support from the original idea. Fever during chemotherapy is an emergency and goes straight to the ward's phone number, not to the daily list.

## Reserve: emergency abdominal surgery

If a seventh group is needed:
- **TWIST RCT** (492 patients after emergency abdominal surgery): wound photos and symptom reports by smartphone on days 3, 7 and 15. Wound infections were **3.7× more likely to be found within the first 7 days** (OR 3.7, p = 0.043), with fewer visits to community care. A third of patients never used the tool. — npj Digital Medicine 2021. [PMC8602321](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8602321/)
- It also supports the wound-photo feature in the hip/knee template.

## 3. Older adults on many medicines (cross-cutting)

- 19% of medical patients had an adverse event within weeks of discharge. **66% of these involved medicines**, and many were preventable or could have been made less severe with simple measures. — Forster et al., *Ann Intern Med* 2003;138:161–7. [PubMed 12558354](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12558354/)
- Drug-related readmissions: median 21% of readmissions (range 3–64%). A median **69%** of them were preventable. — *J Am Geriatr Soc* 2018 systematic review. [PubMed 29468640](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29468640/)
- A stricter meta-analysis of older adults: 9% have a drug-related readmission, up to 22% of them preventable (only 4 studies). — *Drugs & Aging* 2023. [Link](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s40266-023-01076-8)
- Many medicines, older age and other illnesses are the known risk factors. — [PMC7522062](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7522062/)

**For zDoma:** pill reminders, "Vzeto" logging and family notifications aren't side features. They target the most common harm after discharge.

## Not chosen as the main niche

- **Sepsis survivors:** sepsis accounts for 12.2% of Medicare readmissions (heart failure 6.7%, pneumonia 5%), and the 30-day rate is about 20%. There's no clear home signal to monitor, and no strong evidence for home monitoring. — Chang, Fine, Yende, *JAMA* 2017 (DOI 10.1001/jama.2016.20468); [PMC4590016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4590016)
- **Hip/knee replacement as the *main* niche:** readmission rates are lower than for the heart groups. It stays as one of the three templates (section 1c), not the demo story.

## What failed before, and how zDoma answers it

Judges may ask why this would work when earlier telemonitoring trials didn't.

| Weakness found in research | zDoma's answer |
|---|---|
| Neutral trials (Tele-HF, TIM-HF) where many patients **stopped using** the system | Very simple check-in (4 taps, <60 s), reminders, and family members who see missed check-ins |
| Benefit **disappears when monitoring stops** (TIM-HF2 follow-up) | A permanent account with no end date. The rehabilitacija can stay active as long as the bolničar wants |
| Telemedicine centres need staff around the clock | Rule-based triage shows staff only the patients who need action, once a day. Green patients stay hidden |
| No effect on all-cause hospitalisation (Cochrane) | Be honest in the pitch: the claim is fewer **heart-failure** admissions and deaths, not fewer admissions overall |
| Expensive devices (TIM-HF2 used ECG and blood pressure devices) | Uses a household bathroom scale and the patient's phone |
